# Contribution ladder

Roles grow with sustained, reviewed work. The ladder is public so that everyone can see the path.

| Level | Name | How you reach it | What you can do |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | Explorer | Open an issue or join a discussion | Ask questions, report bugs, claim `level:starter` issues |
| 1 | Contributor | One merged pull request | Listed in the contributors graph, claim `level:intermediate` issues |
| 2 | Trusted Contributor | Five merged pull requests over at least two months, including one non-trivial change in a track | Triage issues, be requested as a reviewer, claim `level:advanced` issues |
| 3 | Area Steward | Three months of steady ownership of a track, then nomination | Review and merge inside the track paths, shape the track backlog |
| 4 | Core Maintainer | Nomination and a majority vote of Core Maintainers | Decide architecture, releases, governance |

## Principles

- Quality counts more than volume. Five careful reviews outweigh ten rushed pull requests.
- Reviews count as contributions.
- Every level can contribute in any track. Levels do not gate who may help.
- Counts are guides. A maintainer may promote earlier for exceptional work, and records the reason.
- Emeritus status is available after 90 days without activity. See [GOVERNANCE.md](../GOVERNANCE.md).

## Moving up

1. Keep a record of your work. Link merged pull requests and reviews.
2. Ask a maintainer in your track for feedback.
3. A maintainer nominates you in an issue labeled `type:maintenance`.
4. The nomination follows the rules in [GOVERNANCE.md](../GOVERNANCE.md).

## Area Steward duties

- Triage the track backlog weekly.
- Respond to pull requests inside the review window.
- Keep the track guide accurate.
- Mentor new contributors.
- Hand over cleanly if you step down.
